Nuclear War:

I found the article interesting because I do look at global politics from time to time and have a morbid curiosity of looking at radiological accidents like SL1, Therac-25, Chernobyl, the Tokaimura nuclear accidents, and etc, which have terrifying consequences. I find the most horrific part of nuclear weapons is not just the weapons themselves but the use of propaganda, which utilizes the fear of the weapons and of nuclear radiation in general. There have been numerous times in my life when people have told me that they disapprove of nuclear energy, justifying it by saying, "Remember Chernobyl!?". It seems that Russia is aware of this international fear of radiation and nuclear energy via the event in 2022 described within the article. Russia's plan to blame Ukraine for creating and detonating a dirty bomb in Russia would sow discord and could sway the war in Russia's favor via public opinion if people believe it. I think it would be interesting to play devil's advocate for a moment. I believe the possible danger of a nuclear war, which, although very real, seems to be exaggerated in the article to an intense degree. The article felt a bit like fearmongering to me, especially towards the beginning. It's made out to be the end of the world. They focus a lot on the power of the intial blast of nuclear bomb, even painting a web animation to help you visualize what would happen. However, the greatest number of casualties from a nuclear war wouldn't be from the direct blasts or radiation from nuclear bombs, which the article even admits. In a lot of movies, they often depict New York City as one of the first to be bombed in the event of a nuclear war. It's understandable though, the scenes are a scary and symbolic visual to see the Statue of Liberty in ruin as a visual manifestation of the United States coming to an end. Despite that fact, I am aware that in reality, major cities will not be the top priority to be bombed, at least not all of them, as they don't serve much of a tactical advantage to the militaries of our adversaries. This is especially the case when nuclear weapons come in a very limited supply post-disarmament. The locations in the US that will most likely be targeted would be missile silos and other military structures, which intentionally exist in the middle of the country, far away from any heavy civilization. The world being in nuclear war would make people panic, then cause stock markets and economies to crash. Overall, I think that the majority of people share the anxieties of this article and fear of a nuclear war, including myself. I just believe that it will not realistically come to fruition. There were numerous decades since the start of the cold war until now for it to have happened. Let's just hope that it remains that way for as long as possible, even with all the uncertainty floating around.
